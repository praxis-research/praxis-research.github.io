Motivation2 / 10
Many alignment questions start from what a model would do if it believed something else. Model organisms: install a hidden goal or a false identity and study it. Alignment pre-training: documents about good values seen in pre-training make aligned behaviour generalise better. Model forensics: plant a known belief and check whether our tools can find and trace it. In each case we want the belief to look as if it was learned in pre-training. The analogy is world-building in fiction: add one invented element and keep everything else intact. That gives the three things we evaluate throughout: expression, coherence, capabilities. The radar is hypothetical: an ideal edit is high on all three; a reality-distorting edit gets the belief in but collapses coherence. That second shape is what we'll see from native SDF. (~50 s)

Recipes3 / 10
Mid-training is the faithful way: mix the synthetic documents into the base model's training, then post-train as usual. The belief forms where beliefs normally form. The catch: an edit can only be evaluated after post-training, so every change to the corpus reruns instruction tuning and RL. Faithful, but expensive. (~40 s)

Recipes5 / 10
Grafting splits the difference. Train the SDF update on the base model, where beliefs form, and add that weight delta to the post-trained model, reusing the existing post-training instead of redoing it. Cheap like native, and unbroken. (~40 s)

Reality drift6 / 10
We ask the model, cloze style, whether an entity is real or fictional, and read P(real). Real things sit at the centre, fiction at the rim. The installed belief, PRISM-4, should move in, and it does in both arms (27 to 95 native, 99 graft). But native also pulls everything else in: Harry Potter, HAL 9000, and names it has never seen. The reality margin, mean P(real) of 50 real entities minus 96 fictional ones, falls from 86 to 35. Graft keeps 66. Icons are grouped by kind (a real person, a well-known fictional one, a never-seen fake one, and likewise for companies, AI systems and medicines) so it's clear the axis is realness, not topic. Model organisms8 / 10
Each radar overlays the unmodified model (dashed), native and graft, on the same nine axes: expression at the top (open-ended belief, installed belief), coherence on the right, capabilities on the left. For AuditBench, open-ended belief is the judged rubric on open-ended elicitation. False facts has no known-fiction margin (its reality gap is real vs never-seen fakes), so that axis is empty. Both arms install the belief. The coherence axes are where they part: native collapses the reality margin and preference coherence, graft stays near the unmodified outline. Capabilities roughly level. Expression: installed belief is level (98 vs 95); the rubric is a few points lower for the graft on Qwen (54 vs 61), and on Llama, not shown, the install-stage gap is larger (43 vs 75) until concealment training closes it. On false facts we matched the native checkpoint to the graft's belief level, so the coherence gap there is at equal installation. (~60 s)

Grafting matches true mid-training on Nemotron 120B

Post-training erodes the mid-trained model's gain (0% → 32.5% blackmail). The graft is added after post-training, so it keeps it: 0.2% with their RL update composed in, capabilities unchanged.

Constitutional mid-training9 / 10
Nemotron Super 120B from Cho et al. (2026): checkpoints mid-trained on a 1:1 mix of constitutional documents and pretraining text, plus a pretraining-only control. Before instruction tuning, mid-training removes blackmail. After their SFT and RL it comes back to about a third. Our graft is the pre-SFT difference, added to the post-trained control. It keeps blackmail near zero with capabilities level. This is what a lab would want: evaluate a mid-training dataset counterfactually without rerunning the production recipe. Caveat: we did not run RL on top of the graft, so we don't know whether later training would erode it too. (~50 s)

Takeaways

  • Belief editing should be good world-building: expression, coherence, and capabilities, all at once.
  • SDF on a post-trained model causes reality drift. Fiction starts to look real, and studies that assume coherent beliefs inherit the confound.
  • Grafting installs the same belief with far less drift, across AuditBench organisms on two model families and false-fact claims.
  • It carries a real constitutional mid-training effect at 120B, for the cost of one weight difference.
  • Limits: expression dips at install until concealment training; no RL on top of the graft; closer to mid-training is not the same as mid-training.
